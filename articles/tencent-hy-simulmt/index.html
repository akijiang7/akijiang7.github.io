Tencent Hy-SimulMT: open real-time Chinese-English speech translation with voice cloning

Read the full text ↓

Summary

Tencent Hunyuan has released Hy-SimulMT, a 30B-parameter MoE model (about 3B active) for simultaneous speech translation between Chinese and English. It translates while the speaker is still talking, outputs text and speech, and can clone the speaker's voice in the target language. Weights are Apache 2.0.

What happened

The model card frames simultaneous translation as a trade-off. Waiting for more of the sentence improves accuracy but makes listeners wait longer. Hy-SimulMT aims to start translating early without losing meaning. Tencent ships two families: the standard models output only the translation, while the "Interleave" variants output both the source text and the translation. Each comes in BF16, FP8 and GGUF formats. The GitHub repo includes SimulInfer, an inference service with a web page, audio upload, a real-time WebSocket API and optional TTS with voice cloning, plus SimulTrain training tools.

Key details

  • Size: 30B total parameters, roughly 3B active per token (from the "30B-A3B" name).
  • Directions: bidirectional Chinese ↔ English, speech to speech.
  • License: Apache 2.0.
  • Latency and quality (reported by Tencent): on the RealSI long-form benchmark, a StreamLAAL lag of about 3 seconds, the lowest of the systems compared. Its GEMBA quality score beat the open-source models compared and four of five commercial APIs. On RealSI-Sentence it scored 84.7, the highest in Tencent's table. It scored 90.3 on FLEURS, 75.5 on CoVoST2 and 81.2 on ACL6060.
  • Human comparison: in a small test on 18 recordings of about six minutes each, Tencent says quality was on par with certified professional interpreters, often at lower latency. Tencent itself stresses that this applies only to that sample.
  • How to run: clone the Hy-SimulMT repo, then cd SimulInfer and bash deploy.sh --install-server --model tencent/Hy-SimulMT-30B-A3B-Interleave. Requirements are Linux, Python 3.12+, an NVIDIA GPU and vLLM ≥0.31. Speech output and voice cloning need a separately configured TTS service.

What this means for US developers

Open, commercially usable simultaneous interpretation is rare, and this release includes a ready-made streaming server. With about 3B active parameters, a single GPU should serve it at reasonable cost, and the GGUF builds point to lighter local use. Good fits include live captions for China-facing meetings, cross-border customer calls and research on Chinese-language media. Voice cloning is useful but comes with consent and disclosure obligations. Tencent itself recommends starting with low-risk settings and keeping human interpreters where accountability matters.

A Low-Latency, High-Quality Model for Real-Time Simultaneous Translation

Introduction

Hy-SimulMT is a real-time simultaneous translation model developed by Tencent Hunyuan, supporting bidirectional speech-to-speech translation between Chinese and English. It continuously receives incoming audio and translates as the speaker talks, producing both translated text and speech. With real-time voice cloning, it preserves the speaker's vocal characteristics while conveying the message accurately.

Designed for live communication settings such as meetings, speeches, and interviews, Hy-SimulMT aims to convey information accurately while reducing the disruption that waiting introduces into a conversation. To evaluate this balance between translation quality and response speed, we combine public benchmarks, real-world audio, and comparisons with professional simultaneous interpreters.

In our RealSI long-form audio evaluation, Hy-SimulMT achieved a StreamLAAL of approximately 3 seconds, the lowest among the systems shown in the comparison. Its GEMBA score exceeded those of the compared open-source models and four of the five commercial APIs.

The GitHub repository provides the SimulInfer inference service and SimulTrain training tools. Speech output and voice cloning require a configured TTS service. You can also try Hy-SimulMT in the Hy Translate App.

Results

Keeping Up Faster, Conveying Meaning More Accurately

The challenge of simultaneous translation is not simply to convert one language into another, but to understand and formulate a translation while the speaker is still talking. Waiting for more information often helps resolve the complete meaning, but also makes listeners wait longer. Speaking earlier, on the other hand, places greater demands on incremental understanding and translation generation.

Hy-SimulMT focuses on this balance between quality and latency: translating as a continuous audio stream arrives, so that listeners receive information promptly while the original meaning is preserved as accurately as possible.

We compared Hy-SimulMT with several open-source simultaneous translation models and commercial APIs on the open-source RealSI long-form audio benchmark. The results show that Hy-SimulMT achieves competitive translation quality at lower latency.

The horizontal axis shows StreamLAAL, a measure of streaming translation lag, in seconds (lower is better); the vertical axis shows the GEMBA translation quality score (higher is better). StreamLAAL is not the same as the time to first speech output.

In this evaluation, Hy-SimulMT achieved a StreamLAAL of approximately 3 seconds, the lowest among the systems shown. Its GEMBA score exceeded those of the compared open-source models and four of the five commercial APIs.

These results suggest that lower latency does not necessarily require a substantial sacrifice in translation quality. Hy-SimulMT offers a more timely way to access cross-language information during extended speeches, interviews, and meetings.

Hy-SimulMT scored higher than the other open-source models in the table on FLEURS, ACL6060, and RealSI-Sentence. Its 84.7 on RealSI-Sentence was the highest among all listed systems. Results across these benchmarks also help us identify the model's strengths and areas for improvement.

A Matched Comparison with Professional Simultaneous Interpreters

To further examine performance on real-world content, we collected 18 Chinese and English audio recordings newly appearing online from September 2026 onward, each approximately 6 minutes long. The recordings cover speeches, interviews, and meetings across ten domains: software and AI, medicine, manufacturing and energy, natural sciences, agriculture and food, business management, law, education, culture and tourism, and sports and daily life.

Alongside Hy-SimulMT, we invited six interpreters with years of practical simultaneous interpreting experience to interpret these recordings. Three held CATTI Level 1 interpreting certificates, and three held CATTI Level 2 interpreting certificates. For each certification level, the highest score among the three interpreters was used for comparison.

As this small-scale capability assessment shows, Hy-SimulMT achieves translation quality on par with expert human simultaneous interpreters, while often responding faster (with lower latency).

These findings apply only to the samples, participating interpreters, and evaluation settings used in this test. They do not constitute a comprehensive assessment of speech quality, voice similarity, or other dimensions.

Beyond Text: Preserving the Speaker's Voice

Cross-language communication conveys not only information, but also the characteristics of a speaker's voice. Hy-SimulMT supports real-time voice cloning, preserving the original speaker's vocal characteristics while generating speech in the target language.

Continuing to Improve Through Real-World Feedback

Simultaneous translation ultimately serves real communication. Beyond benchmark scores, we value professional interpreters' feedback on information delivery, response timing, and how natural the translated speech sounds.

---

Deploy inference

Follow the SimulInfer README for step-by-step installation, or use the one-command deployment below. With --install-server, the command installs dependencies in isolated deployment environments. Running it again repeats the installation steps and may reinstall SimulInfer or change dependency versions in those environments. If you use a custom vLLM/CUDA environment or need control over dependency versions, follow the step-by-step guide or use the Existing compatible vLLM environment option below.

SimulInfer provides a web page, audio file uploads, real-time WebSocket and optional TTS with input voice cloning. The deployed model determines whether source text is displayed; no output-mode selection is needed.

vLLM

Prepare a Linux machine with Python 3.12+, Git, an NVIDIA GPU with enough memory for the selected model, and a CUDA-compatible driver. For an existing model environment, use vLLM >=0.31 with HYV3ForCausalLM support and install simulst.

Clone the project and enter the inference directory. Run the deployment commands below from SimulInfer/:

git clone https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hy-SimulMT.git Hy-SimulMT
cd Hy-SimulMT
cd SimulInfer

New environment — install dependencies and start both services:

bash deploy.sh --install-server --model tencent/Hy-SimulMT-30B-A3B-Interleave

For an accessible local checkpoint, replace the model ID:

bash deploy.sh --install-server --model /path/to/Hy-SimulMT-30B-A3B-Interleave

The command installs dependencies in separate environments, prepares the model, starts vLLM, then starts the web/API service. The first run downloads dependencies and, when using a Hugging Face ID, the model. Keep the command running.

Existing compatible vLLM environment:

/path/to/model-env/bin/python -m pip install --no-deps simulst
bash deploy.sh --server-python /path/to/model-env/bin/python --model /path/to/model

This uses your existing model environment and creates a separate environment for the web service. For custom vLLM/CUDA builds, use this option.

Already running a model API:

bash deploy.sh --backend-url http://model-host:8800/v1

This starts only SimulInfer. See the model setup instructions if the existing model needs its inference template prepared.

Open and use

Wait for Ready: http://127.0.0.1:8080/, then open that address on the server, or http://SERVER_IP:8080/ from another machine. Select a translation direction, upload audio or start the microphone, then click Stop and finish when done. Remote microphone access requires HTTPS; use your HTTPS port-forwarding URL if available, including any /proxy/8080/ prefix.

Upload a file from the command line:

curl -N http://127.0.0.1:8080/translate/stream \
  -F direction=en-zh -F file=@audio.wav

Or open another terminal in Hy-SimulMT/SimulInfer/ and use the installed client:

.simulinfer/app-env/bin/simulinfer-client audio.wav --url http://127.0.0.1:8080

When TTS is enabled in the deployment configuration, the same command also saves the returned speech to outputs/translated.wav. /docs provides API documentation.

Configure and stop

Defaults: web/API port 8080, local model port 8800. Files are saved under SimulInfer/.simulinfer/, including config.toml, app.log and model.log when a model is started. Press Ctrl+C to stop the services started by the command. An occupied port causes a startup error; select another port with --port or --model-port.

For a saved deployment configuration:

cp deploy.example.toml deploy.toml
# Edit the model, GPU, ports and optional TTS connection.
bash deploy.sh --config deploy.toml

Examples:

bash deploy.sh --install-server --model /path/to/model --gpus 0
bash deploy.sh --install-server --model /path/to/model --gpus 0,1 --tensor-parallel-size 2
bash deploy.sh --backend-url http://model-host:8800/v1 --port 8081 --runtime-dir .simulinfer-alt

Use SIMULINFER_PYTHON=python3.12 before the command if python3 is not the Python version you want. Reuse the same deployment command to start it again. The detailed guide covers installation and deployment options, configuration, audio clients, and TTS setup.

llama.cpp

We added architecture-specific support for Hy-SimulMT to llama.cpp to enable inference with GGUF models. We have submitted PR #31198 to the llama.cpp repository.

This support has not yet been merged into the official llama.cpp repository. The PR currently supports inference through llama-mtmd-cli; llama-server support is planned as a follow-up. Before using the server deployment steps below, ensure that your llama.cpp version includes the required Hy-SimulMT server support.

Use this option when an audio-capable llama.cpp server has already loaded your Hy-SimulMT GGUF model on port 8080. In a Python 3.11+ environment, run the following from the repository root:

cd SimulInfer
python -m pip install .
export MODEL=/path/to/hymt-A3b-tgt_only-1019-Q8_0.gguf
MODEL_TYPE=target_only python serve_llamacpp.py

Replace MODEL with your own GGUF path, exactly as loaded by the llama.cpp server. Use MODEL_TYPE=target_only for a translation-only model, or source_target for a model that outputs both source text and translation.

For serve_llamacpp.py, open http://127.0.0.1:8094/ for the web page. This is the bridge web/API port; 8080 is the default llama.cpp model port. The separate deploy.sh / simulinfer launch path defaults to web/API port 8080. For the llama.cpp bridge, file uploads and WebSocket use /translate/stream and /translate/ws on port 8094. The script connects to http://127.0.0.1:8080 by default; set LLAMA_URL for a different model server and PORT for a different web/API port. For remote microphone access, use an HTTPS port-forwarding address.

SGLang-Omni

X-post

SGLang-Omni is a multi-stage serving runtime that serves Hy-SimulMT through OpenAI-compatible HTTP endpoints and a realtime WebSocket. It covers this checkpoint (Hy-SimulMT-30B-A3B, BF16, translation-only output). It returns translated text only: speech output and voice cloning require a separate TTS service, and the FP8, GGUF and Interleave checkpoints are not covered by this integration.

Requirements: Linux with an NVIDIA GPU and a CUDA driver. The BF16 weights occupy about 57 GiB before KV cache and runtime allocations, so a single H200 is sufficient. Docker is recommended, because UCX, flash-attn, SGLang and CUDA are prebuilt:

docker run -it \
    --shm-size 32g \
    --gpus all \
    --ipc host \
    --network host \
    --privileged \
    hongccc/sglang-omni:dev \
    /bin/zsh

Inside the container, install sglang-omni:

git clone https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang-omni.git
cd sglang-omni

pip install --upgrade pip
pip install uv

uv venv .venv -p 3.12
source .venv/bin/activate

uv pip install --prerelease=allow -v -e .

Start the server:

sgl-omni serve \
  --model-path tencent/Hy-SimulMT-30B-A3B \
  --mem-fraction-static 0.75 \
  --enable-realtime \
  --port 8000

Decode CUDA graphs are enabled and Torch compilation is disabled by default. --enable-realtime is only required for the streaming WebSocket below. The web/API port here is 8000, not the 8080 used by SimulInfer.

For Chinese speech to English text:

curl http://localhost:8000/v1/audio/translations \
  -F file=@chinese.wav \
  -F language=zh \
  -F response_format=json

For English speech to Chinese text:

curl http://localhost:8000/v1/audio/transcriptions \
  -F file=@english.wav \
  -F language=en \
  -F response_format=json

language is the source language (zh or en); pass it explicitly, because this checkpoint provides no language-detection tokens. Omitting it defaults to Chinese-to-English. Each request accepts one file of at most 30 seconds; longer audio is rejected rather than truncated. Add -F stream=true to either endpoint to receive transcript.text.delta SSE events followed by the authoritative transcript.text.done.

Live audio uses /v1/realtime?intent=transcription (requires --enable-realtime) with mono PCM16 at 16 kHz:

  1. Send session.update with session.language set to the source language (zh or en).
  2. Send base64 PCM chunks with input_audio_buffer.append.
  3. Receive transcription.segment translation hypotheses, refreshed every 1.6 seconds.
  4. Send input_audio_buffer.commit to finish a segment, or transcription.done to finish input.

Each partial request keeps earlier audio turns and their translations as context; the final hypothesis re-translates the complete segment and can revise earlier partial translations. The runtime starts a fresh translation context after 30 seconds. This path does not reproduce SimulInfer's VAD and recovery heuristics.

See the Hy-SimulMT cookbook for request parameters, streaming behavior, reference validation against Transformers, and measured latency (up to 6.5x faster than Transformers on H200 at batch size one).

cd SimulTrain
